Learn Entity Resolution with Phoebe

Two systems describe the same customers and nothing joins them. There is no shared id, the emails disagree, and one person appears three times under two surnames. Six sessions build a working matcher over Meridian - 63 records covering 32 real people - and end at the finding that decides how the whole thing should be designed: two pairs that score exactly the same match weight, where one is the same person and one is a father and his son. No threshold separates them, which is why Fellegi and Sunter specified a band for a human and why almost every production system quietly drops it.

What is real here 🧾

A course about judging other people's matching has to be exact about its own.

ClaimStatus
Every similarity score and match weightReal implementations running in your browser - Jaro-Winkler with the prefix capped at 4 and scale 0.1, Levenshtein, Damerau-Levenshtein, Jaro, Jaccard and Soundex. Type two strings into the session 2 lab and the seven numbers recompute as you type
Precision, recall, F1, pair completeness, reduction ratio, cluster countsComputed against the 35 labelled true pairs at the moment you move a control. Nothing is scripted and no figure is looked up
The two pairs that both score 0.84A genuine property of the data, not a staged demonstration. One is Sarah Whitfield after a job change and a move; one is a father and son sharing a landline. Both single-threshold rules measure F1 0.971 and neither can do better
The m and u probability tableA judgement, not a measurement. It stands in for what Splink would estimate with expectation maximisation on a real corpus. Labelled as such on the widget itself. Everything computed from it is real arithmetic
Blocking costs nine percent of the true pairsMeasured on all four schemes. Postcode, surname soundex and sorted neighbourhood all land at 91 percent pair completeness on this data
Master data management's four implementation stylesAttributed to Gartner consistently across five independent vendor sources, with no primary Gartner note obtainable. Taught as an analyst framework, never as an academic citation. "Golden record" has no traceable originating citation either, and "survivorship rules" is vendor vocabulary
Sorted neighbourhood, B-cubed, MinHash, and the PETS 2011 cryptanalysisReal work, cited from secondary sources rather than fetched primaries. Flagged on the page that teaches each
PDPA and entity resolutionNot covered. Session 6 reasons about GDPR identifiability and purpose limitation, and says plainly that no GDPR clause names record linkage and that PDPA was not researched
